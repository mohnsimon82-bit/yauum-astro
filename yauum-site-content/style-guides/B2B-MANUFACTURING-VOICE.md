# YAUUM B2B Manufacturing Voice Guide

## Purpose

Use a direct, helpful voice for overseas brand owners, wholesalers, and sourcing teams. The reader should feel that an experienced production contact is helping them make the next decision.

This guide takes inspiration from the communication rhythm of strong industrial service pages. It does not copy another company’s wording, claims, structure, or brand voice.

## Core voice

- Speak to the buyer as “you” and use “we” only when YAUUM can support the stated action.
- Start with what the buyer wants to make, decide, check, or send.
- Explain technical terms in plain English before adding detail.
- Use active verbs: choose, send, check, compare, confirm, review, approve.
- Keep most sentences between 10 and 20 words.
- Keep most paragraphs between two and four sentences.
- Answer question headings in the first sentence.
- End important sections with a practical next step.

## Preferred pattern

1. Buyer question or goal.
2. Direct answer.
3. The two or three details that change the answer.
4. What the buyer should send or confirm next.

Example:

> What affects your hoodie MOQ?
>
> MOQ depends on the fabric, color, artwork, labels, and size split. Send the quantity you want to test and the quantity you expect to reorder. We can then review the project against the actual specification.

## Avoid

- Abstract phrases such as “connected manufacturing ecosystem” or “commercial solution.”
- Repeating “scope,” “direction,” “workflow,” and “requirements” in every section.
- Long lists inside one sentence.
- Generic claims such as “best quality,” “fast delivery,” or “competitive price.”
- Fixed MOQ, lead time, capacity, price, certification, or quality guarantees without verified evidence.
- Pretending generated product images are real orders.
- Copying sentences or distinctive phrasing from competitor websites.

## Proof rule

Use a number or claim only when YAUUM has a current document, order record, test result, or company source that supports it. When the fact varies by project, say what changes the answer and invite the buyer to send the missing details.

## CTA style

Use specific actions:

- Send your hoodie brief
- Share your tech pack
- Discuss your fabric and GSM
- Review your artwork options
- Prepare your sample request

Avoid vague CTAs such as “Learn More” when a more useful action is available.
