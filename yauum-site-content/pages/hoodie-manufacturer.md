All fleece and French terry are pre-shrunk before cutting. Untreated 380 GSM fleece can shrink 8–10% on first wash. Pre-shrunk treatment brings shrinkage to under 3% — what most retail standards require.

#### French Terry vs Fleece: The Practical Tradeoff

French terry has a looped back, smoother face. Lighter, breathes better, works for layering or variable climates. Fleece (brushed back) is warmer, softer inside, better for standalone hoodies in colder conditions.

For southern US markets: 280–320 GSM French terry performs better — customers don’t need the warmth.

For northern EU and UK: 380–420 GSM fleece handles the climate and the premium price point.

For streetwear/oversized: 380–420 GSM with a relaxed fit — buyers in this segment associate heavier weight with quality.

### How We Keep Quality Consistent From Sample to Bulk — and What Inspection Actually Looks Like

The most common complaint about clothing manufacturers: the bulk order didn’t match the sample.

This usually happens at one of three points:

- Fabric sourcing: Supplier changes fabric source between sample and bulk

- Production variation: Sample runs on different equipment than bulk

- QC gaps: No systematic inspection, just visual check

Here’s how we handle each:

1. Fabric locking: Before we produce your sample, we confirm the fabric spec (GSM, composition, supplier lot). That exact lot is reserved for your bulk order. If the reserved lot runs out, we source from the same supplier with the same spec — not a same-type fabric.

2. Same production line: Your sample and bulk run on the same equipment. We note the production line and shift on your order confirmation.

3. Documented inspection: Here’s what inspection actually looks like at Yauum:

Pre-shipment inspection checkpoints:

- Fabric inspection on arrival — GSM spot check, visual defect scan

- In-line inspection during sewing — stitch density, seam strength, dimensions

- Final inspection before packing — full piece check against approved sample

- Packing inspection — label accuracy, fold quality, polybag integrity

We use AQL 2.5 for final inspection.

#### What AQL 2.5 Means in Plain Terms

AQL (Acceptable Quality Level) is the maximum percentage of defective items considered acceptable in a batch. AQL 2.5 means up to 2.5% of inspected pieces can have minor defects before the batch is rejected.

- For 100 pieces: acceptable defects = 0–2

- For 500 pieces: acceptable defects = 0–12

- For 1,000 pieces: acceptable defects = 0–25

Critical defects — safety issues, wrong label, missing components — result in automatic rejection regardless of AQL.

#### L/C vs T/T: Which Should You Use?

T/T: Direct bank payment. Faster, lower fees, simpler. Best for orders under $10,000 and brands with established trust.

L/C: Bank-guaranteed payment. The buyer’s bank guarantees payment when conditions (shipping documents) are met. For larger orders where you want payment protection. L/C adds 5–7 days processing and $300–$800 in bank fees.
